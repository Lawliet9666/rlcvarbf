Learning to Adapt Risk via Differentiable CVaR Barrier Functions

Xinyi Wang1, Taekyung Kim1, Bardh Hoxha2, Georgios Fainekos2, Dimitra Panagou1,
1Department of Robotics, University of Michigan, 2Toyota North America Research & Development
Paper Video Code

Abstract

In dense dynamic crowds, robots navigate in real time under uncertain obstacle motion. The main challenge is handling dense, uncertain interactions while preserving safety without becoming overly conservative or sacrificing efficiency. To solve this challenge, we propose an end-to-end risk-adaptive framework that integrates reinforcement learning with a differentiable CVaR-BF-QP safety layer under Gaussian-mixture motion uncertainty. The policy jointly outputs nominal control, risk level, and safety margin, while the safety layer enforces probabilistic constraints and remains differentiable for gradient-based training. This joint design lets the robot act cautiously in advance when interactions tighten, and relax conservatism only when needed to keep the QP feasible. We evaluate both single-integrator and unicycle robots in dynamic crowd simulations with up to 20 moving obstacles, and assess generalization under out-of-distribution crowd shifts. Compared with three type of policy: optimization, RL, and RL with optimization policies, the proposed method achieves higher safety and efficiency with more robust performance.

Motivation

(a) A fixed risk level is not flexible enough: A low risk tolerance enhances safety but can render the optimization infeasible, whereas a high risk tolerance improves feasibility at the expense of safety.
(b) In highly dynamic scenarios, where obstacles move unpredictably and rapidly, the robot requires sufficient time and space to respond and adjust its risk level.
(c) Our method dynamically adjusts the risk level within an extended risk range to maintain feasibility while ensuring user-defined probabilistic safety. The robot proactively modifies its trajectory before approaching obstacles, but only when necessary, thus avoiding unnecessary conservatism.

Statistical Results

Q1: How do different method types compare in dynamic environments?

Optimization-based methods generally struggle in highly dynamic environments due to frequent infeasibility of the underlying control problems, resulting in lower success rates.

In contrast, pure RL methods improve task performance in some cases but still exhibit relatively high collision rates without explicit safety mechanisms, indicating that efficiency alone is insufficient. Hybrid RL with optimization approaches leverage the strengths of both types of methods and achieve more reliable performance overall, highlighting the benefit of combining learning with explicit safety mechanisms.

Q2: How do different ways of integrating safety into RL affect performance?

Incorporating safety into RL policies generally improves performance compared to pure RL methods. These results indicate that post-hoc safety filtering is effective in enhancing policy reliability.

However, such decoupled approaches remain suboptimal compared to methods that integrate safety directly into the training process. In contrast, the proposed method and BarrierNet jointly optimize the policy and safety constraints through a differentiable safety layer, enabling adaptive risk-aware behavior.

Unlike BarrierNet, which employs a deterministic differentiable safety layer, the proposed method explicitly models stochastic uncertainty and learns adaptive risk parameters, leading to improved performance in dynamic and uncertain environments.

Q3: How robust are the methods across different robot models and obstacle densities?

Similar performance trends are observed for both the single-integrator and unicycle models. While all methods experience some degradation under the more challenging unicycle dynamics, this is expected due to its more constrained control dynamics.

The relative performance ordering remains largely consistent across robot models. As obstacle density grows, all methods exhibit a clear decline in success rate, reflecting the increased difficulty of navigation in dense environments.

However, the rate of degradation varies across methods: hybrid approaches are comparatively more stable, while other methods degrade more rapidly. Notably, the proposed method consistently outperforms all baselines across both robot models and varying obstacle densities, demonstrating strong robustness.

Q4: How robust are different type of methods under out-of-distribution (OOD) environment changes?

Under OOD shifts, vanilla RL exhibits a noticeable degradation in performance, often performing worse than optimization-based methods such as CVaR-BF-QP. This suggests that optimization-based approaches provide greater robustness to environmental variations compared to purely learned policies.

At the same time, incorporating optimization-based safety mechanisms into RL, such as RL + Safety Filter, significantly mitigates this degradation, recovering much of the lost performance and bringing it closer to that of optimization-based methods, although a performance gap still remains.

To further address this gap, the proposed method consistently outperforms the safety-filtered baseline, achieving higher success rates and returns while maintaining competitive safety. This robustness arises from the end-to-end learning of adaptive risk parameters, which enables the policy to adjust its behavior in response to changing scenarios.
